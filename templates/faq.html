FAQ

Common questions

The short answers. For the longer ones, see the docs.

Running it

Do I need Home Assistant?

No. It also runs as a plain Docker container behind any reverse proxy, or as an ordinary Python app. Home Assistant is simply the easiest path: one-click install, updates in the usual place, and secure remote access with nothing exposed. If you do go that route you need Home Assistant OS or Supervised — add-ons aren't available on Container or Core.

What hardware do I need?

Very little. A Raspberry Pi 4 is plenty, and that's what it was built on — the app is a small Flask service with a single-file database, and the heavy lifting happens on the model provider's hardware. Anything that runs Docker will do.

Can I try it before committing to an API key?

Yes. Set the provider to mock and the whole app works with canned recipes and no outbound calls at all — stock, suggestions, your own recipes, everything except real generation. It's the honest way to see whether you'd use it.

Can I use it when I'm not at home?

Through Home Assistant, yes — with Nabu Casa or your own HTTPS it's reachable over HA's authenticated ingress, with no ports forwarded. Standalone, that's down to your own reverse proxy and TLS setup.

What happens to my data when I update?

Database changes are applied automatically on start, and they're written to be additive so existing recipes and history carry through. Configure Home Assistant's backups anyway before a version that changes the schema — the release notes say when that's the case.

Cost & privacy

What does it cost to run?

The app is free and open source. The only running cost is your provider's API usage on your own key, and only generation calls the model — browsing, filtering, stock and cookable suggestions are all local and free. One generation is a single call to a lightweight model, so a month of normal household cooking is pennies. Set a spending cap in your provider's console and you can't be surprised.

Does my data leave my network?

Your stock, recipes, history, notes and accounts stay on your hardware. The only thing sent out is a short generation brief — ingredients and constraints — to your chosen model, and only when you press generate. The exact contents are listed on Privacy & data.

Can I run it entirely locally, with no cloud model?

Not yet, and I'd rather say so than imply otherwise. The provider adapter is built to be swappable and a local model such as Ollama is the obvious next one to add, but it isn't written. Today, real generation means a hosted model and an API key. Everything else is already local.

Which model does it use, and can I change it?

A lightweight hosted model by default, through a swappable adapter — Gemini or Anthropic, with your own key, plus a keyless mock. The specific model is a setting, so you can point it at a cheaper or a stronger one without touching code.

Using it

Do I have to type in every ingredient I own?

No. A fresh install comes with around 170 common ingredients already in the catalogue, and anything you always have — oil, salt, spices, flour — gets flagged as a staple and dropped from the stock list entirely. What's left to keep up with is the fresh stuff that genuinely comes and goes, as a tap-in, tap-out list.

Can more than one person use it?

Yes. Everyone gets their own sign-in, their own favourites and their own dietary tags. Recipes and notes are shared across the household — you see what everyone's cooked. Pick who's eating and the brief adjusts to them.

Can I add my own recipes?

Yes, and it's worth doing. Write them straight into the app with ingredients picked from your catalogue, and they join your cookable suggestions exactly like generated ones. They're often the ones you'll actually be offered, since they're the food you already cook.

Can it import a recipe from a website or a photo of a cookbook?

Not yet — both are next on the list. Web import is the nearer of the two, since most recipe sites publish structured data that can be read in directly. For now, recipes are typed in by hand.

Why aren't any recipes showing up in my suggestions?

Almost always because the bank is still small — suggestions only offer recipes you can cook right now, so they're sparse at first and grow with your history. If a specific recipe won't appear, check it has a meal type tag and that each of its ingredients is linked to a catalogue entry; anything unverifiable is left out rather than promised falsely.

Is it safe for allergies?

Allergies are stated in the brief as absolute exclusions and the model handles the obvious cases well, but an AI is not a safety guarantee and the app can't verify the output in code. Recipes generated with an allergy in play carry a visible reminder to check the ingredients yourself. Always do.

Does it do shopping lists, quantities or meal planning?

No, and deliberately not. The app is for cooking with what you've got, and each of those would pull it towards being an inventory system to maintain. The reasoning is written up under Approach.

Can I get my recipes back out?

Your data is a single SQLite file on your own machine, which you can copy, query or back up however you like — nothing is locked away in a proprietary format or on someone else's server. Individual recipes print or save to PDF from the share view. There's no one-click bulk export button today.
