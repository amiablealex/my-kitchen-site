What this is

  • {{ icon('shelves') }}

    Stock manager

    Designed for minimal effort. Check ingredients in and out, grouped and colour-coded by section; set up a bank of staples like oil and spices and My Kitchen assumes they're always there. In or out; not an inventory to keep accurate.

  • {{ icon('kitchen') }}

    Your own recipe bank

    Write your own recipes, linking each ingredient to your catalogue. That structure is the engine behind working out what's cookable.

  • {{ icon('search') }}

    See what you can make right now

    It knows what's in stock, asks what kind of thing you're after, then surfaces every saved recipe that fits and that you have the ingredients for.

  • {{ icon('cooking') }}

    Creative assistant

    If nothing takes your fancy, an AI model takes the brief and returns two deliberately different recipes to choose from, built around what's in stock, with techniques and finishing touches that make them worth cooking.

  • {{ icon('group') }}

    Everyone in the house

    Separate logins and favourites, shared recipes and notes. Record allergies, dislikes and diets per person; pick who's eating and the brief adjusts. Leave notes on a recipe for next time — what you changed, whether to make it again.

  • {{ icon('lock') }}

    Self-hosted and private

    Your stock, recipes, history and accounts never leave your hardware. The only thing that does is a short ingredient brief to the AI model, and only when you generate. More on data ›

A look inside

Built small, on purpose

My Kitchen does one job: turning what you've got into something worth cooking. It isn't an inventory system, a shopping-list app, a meal planner or a nutrition tracker — and the reasons those were left out are written down.

{{ icon('tune') }} What it won't do, and why

Run it on your own hardware

You'll need somewhere to host it. The easiest route by far is as a Home Assistant app; otherwise it's an ordinary Docker container, or a plain Python app.
