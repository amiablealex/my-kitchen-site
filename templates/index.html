What it does

My Kitchen is a simple stock manager, personal recipe database, and creative assistant. It shows you which of your personal recipe database you can cook right now, based on what you're feeling like and what you've got currently in the kitchen. None of the recipes take your fancy? An integrated AI model takes your brief and generates a high-quality creative recipe - verified against your live in-stock database to make sure you'll actually be able to cook it.

  • {{ icon('shelves') }}

    Simple stock inventory manager

    Designed to be effortless and minimise the maintenance burden. Configure a bank of staples like oil and spices, and My Kitchen assumes they'll always be available.

  • {{ icon('kitchen') }}

    Comprehensive recipe database

    Upload your own recipes, and link each ingredient to your real catalogue. This is the engine that lets the app work out what's cookable.

  • {{ icon('search') }}

    See what you can make right now

    At “Lets cook”, My Kitchen knows what you've got in stock, asks what kind of recipe you're after (e.g breakfast, dinner, snack) and optionally a cuisine (british, mediterranean...), then surfaces all your saved recipes that fit the brief.

  • {{ icon('cooking') }}

    Fresh ideas when you want them

    Nothing take your fancy? An integrated AI model takes the brief and a creative angle, then returns two distinctly different recipes for you to choose from that you can make with what you've got in stock right now.

  • {{ icon('group') }}

    Built for the household

    Add everyone to the app, and you can all see each other's favourite recipes. Configure your own dietary needs, allergies and preferences, choose who you're cooking for and any generated recipes adjust accordingly. Always check allergies yourself, because an AI is never a safety net.

  • {{ icon('lock') }}

    Yours, on your own hardware

    Self-hosted and private: your stock, recipes and history never leave your network. Only a short ingredient brief goes to the AI model when you generate, and a local-model option even keeps that local. More on data ›

A look inside

Run it on your own hardware

You'll need a device to host the server. The easiest option is a Home Assistant (HAOS) App. Otherwise, you'll need to configure a docker container. Either way, it's your kitchen, on your network.
