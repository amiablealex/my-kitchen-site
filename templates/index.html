One input among several — not the whole product

Most recipe apps are a search box bolted to an AI. My Kitchen is built the other way round: every recipe is linked to your real ingredient catalogue, so the app can tell you what you can cook right now before it ever calls a model. AI generation is there when you want something new — verified against your kitchen, then handed to you to review.

What it does

{{ icon('shelves') }}

Cook from what you have

A stock list that stays maintained because it's a single tap — in or out, never an inventory to manage. The wizard builds meals around what's actually in.

{{ icon('kitchen') }}

A pantry that's actually linked

Every ingredient on every recipe is matched to your catalogue — so the app can reason about what's cookable, not just match free text.

{{ icon('search') }}

Suggestions before you generate

At “Lets cook”, see saved recipes you can make tonight from current stock — surfaced first, so a great meal you've already made doesn't get forgotten.

{{ icon('restaurant_menu') }}

Two recipes, you pick

Generation returns two genuinely different ideas with an energetic write-up — the why, the technique, the finishing touches — then you choose.

{{ icon('group') }}

Built for the household

Multiple people, individual dietary needs and allergies, per-person favourites. Pick who you're cooking for and the brief adjusts.

How it works

  1. 1

    Keep stock current

    Tap items in or out as you shop and cook. Staples like oil and spices are assumed — you only track the things that come and go.

  2. 2

    Lets cook

    A quick guided flow: pick anything you want to use up, choose a meal type, time and who you're feeding. One screen at a time, one-handed at the hob.

  3. 3

    Cook now, or make something new

    See saved recipes you can cook from current stock. Nothing takes your fancy? Generate two fresh ideas, built around your kitchen and constraints.

  4. 4

    Cook, save, favourite

    Clear four-part recipes — intro, ingredients, prep, cook. Favourite the keepers; they feed straight back into tomorrow's suggestions.

A look inside

Run it on your own hardware

The easiest path is a one-click Home Assistant add-on. Prefer plain Docker? That works too. Either way, it's your kitchen, on your network.
