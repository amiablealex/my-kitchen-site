{{ site.name }}

A recipe app for one household, self-hosted and open source.

Upload all your recipes from books and the web, keep track of what's in stock, and My Kitchen will show you which of your recipes are cookable with what you've got in.

Go through the Let's Cook wizard to tell it what you fancy; if no existing recipe fits the brief My Kitchen asks an AI model to generate one from what you've got.

An integrated AI assistant lets you ask questions about your recipes, generate new versions based on feedback, and helps make keeping stock and importing recipes effortless.

Features

  • {{ icon('shelves') }}

    Simple Stock Tracking

    Each ingredient is either in or out, grouped by section. Configure staples like oil and spices to make them always available. Paste a shopping list or import a receipt photo, and mark what a recipe used up when you've cooked it.

  • {{ icon('menu_book') }}

    Recipe Database

    Paste a link to a recipe, paste its text, or upload a photo of a cookbook page or a handwritten card. The app arranges it into a standard form, for you to check before saving. Ingredient lines are linked to your ingredient catalogue, which is how the app knows which of your recipes are cookable right now.

  • {{ icon('search') }}

    Cooking from what's in

    The Let's Cook wizard asks what you want to use up, the cuisine, and effort level, then lists which of your saved recipes fit the brief.

  • New recipes

    If nothing fits, you get the option to send the brief to your AI model along with a request, such as “something spicy”. It returns two varied recipes for you to add to your database, or you can ask for two more.

  • {{ icon('edit') }}

    Versions

    Ask for a change (“make it for four”, “add some vegetables”) on any recipe, your own and imported ones included, and the app writes a new version: from what's in stock, or with free rein. Earlier versions stay in a small tree on the recipe, you can branch from any of them, and nothing is sent until you confirm.

  • {{ icon('comment') }}

    Questions in the notes

    Start a note with @kitchen to ask about that recipe, and the reply arrives in the thread. If it suggests a bigger change, one tap turns it into a new version.

  • {{ icon('group') }}

    Household

    Each person has a login, favourites and dietary tags: allergies, dislikes and diets. Choose who's eating and their tags go into the brief.

  • {{ icon('lock') }}

    Your data

    Everything is stored in one SQLite file on your machine. Only what an AI request needs leaves your network, sent to your provider with your key, and photos are never kept. Privacy & data

Runs on
Home Assistant (as an add-on), Docker, or Python.
Stack
Flask, SQLite and server-rendered templates.
AI
Google Gemini or Anthropic with your own API key, or (untested) your own local model through an OpenAI-compatible server such as Ollama. Used to write and revise recipes, answer questions, read receipts and lay out imported recipes; everything else runs locally. The mock provider lets you run it without a key.
Cost
Free, MIT licence. API use is billed to your key: pennies a month for a household, with daily limits you set.
