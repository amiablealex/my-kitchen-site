What it does

Most recipe apps are a search box bolted to an AI. My Kitchen works the other way round: it starts from what's actually in your kitchen, shows you what you can cook right now, and only reaches for the model when you want something new — verified against your stock, then handed to you to review. AI is one ingredient here, not the whole dish.

  • {{ icon('shelves') }}

    Cook from what's actually in

    Keep stock with a single tap as you shop and cook — in or out, never an inventory to manage. Staples like oil and spices are assumed, so you only track the things that come and go, and meals get built around what you have.

  • {{ icon('search') }}

    See what you can make right now

    At “Lets cook”, My Kitchen surfaces saved recipes you can cook tonight from current stock — before it generates anything. A great meal you've made before doesn't get forgotten, and you skip a needless trip to the model.

  • {{ icon('cooking') }}

    Fresh ideas when you want them

    Nothing takes your fancy? Generate. It builds a brief from your kitchen, your constraints and a creative angle, then returns two genuinely different recipes — the why, the technique, the finishing touches. You pick one; both are saved.

  • {{ icon('kitchen') }}

    Every recipe linked to your kitchen

    Each ingredient line is matched to your real catalogue, not loose text — which is what lets the app reason about what's cookable in the first place. Write your own recipes in too, and re-link anything by hand whenever you like.

  • {{ icon('group') }}

    Built for the whole household

    Add everyone who eats here, each with their own dietary needs, allergies and favourites. Choose who you're cooking for and the recipe adjusts — with a clear reminder to check allergies yourself, because an AI is never a safety net.

  • {{ icon('lock') }}

    Yours, on your own hardware

    Self-hosted and private: your stock, recipes and history never leave your network. Only a short ingredient brief goes to the model when you generate — and a local-model option keeps even that in-house. More on data ›
