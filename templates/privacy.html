Privacy & data

What stays on your machine, and what's sent to your AI provider and when.

On your machine

Accounts, stock, the ingredient catalogue, recipes, history, favourites and notes are all in one SQLite database file on the machine running My Kitchen. There's no account with me, no sync, no telemetry and no analytics, and nothing is shared between households.

Sent to your AI provider

The app calls your chosen provider, with your own API key, in the situations below and no others. Browsing, filtering, suggestions and ordinary stock changes never leave your network.

New recipes

When you tap Generate recipes (or ask for two more), the request contains:

  • the ingredients: your must-use picks, what's in stock, your staples, and any notes on them;
  • your equipment list;
  • the meal type, cuisine, time band and number of servings;
  • the dietary tags of the people you're cooking for, by tag name, not by person;
  • your request, if you wrote one;
  • the titles of your recent recipes, and of the two you passed on if you asked for two more;
  • a randomly chosen creative angle.

New versions

When you confirm a new version, the request contains the current recipe in full, the change you asked for, and the same kind of information as above for the original meal.

@kitchen questions

When you ask @kitchen, the request contains the recipe, your question, the servings you're viewing it at, the last few questions and answers on that recipe, what's in stock, your staples, and the allergy and dislike tags of everyone in the household (tag names only).

Receipts

A receipt can carry the shop's name and address, the date and time, your card's last digits, and your loyalty card number, so the app makes you frame the item lines before anything is sent. The crop and a size reduction happen on your phone, so only the framed part leaves it, and not even your own server sees the rest. That crop goes to your provider for one read, with your ingredient catalogue (names only) so it can match the items. It isn't written to disk or logged, and the reply is checked against your catalogue before anything is shown.

If you'd rather not send receipt photos, paste the receipt's text into the stock search instead, or turn receipt scanning off with receipt_scanning.

Pasted lists

A list pasted into the stock search is matched on your own machine first. Only the lines it can't place are sent, as text, with your ingredient catalogue's names.

Imported recipes

For a link, the app fetches the page itself and sends the recipe's structured data where the site provides it, or the page's text where it doesn't. Pasted recipe text is sent as written. For photos, the pages you add are shrunk on your phone, sent together, and not kept.

Account details, people's names, favourites and ordinary notes are never sent.

A small machine like a Raspberry Pi can't run a model that writes recipes this well, so by default the AI features use a cloud model while everything else stays local. I'd rather say that plainly than call the app fully private.

Dietary tags are free text you write yourself. If you name one after a medical condition, that wording goes into requests; naming tags after foods rather than diagnoses keeps it out.

Running fully local

Possible, but untested. The openai_compatible provider talks to a model server on your own network, such as Ollama or LM Studio, and then nothing leaves it: generation, @kitchen, imports, list matching and, with a model that reads images, receipts. I haven't been able to test it, and it will only be as good as your model; small models are less reliable with allergy rules. See Your own model.

mock mode also runs the whole app with no API key and no outbound calls. You lose generation, @kitchen, receipts and imports, but stock, suggestions, your own recipes and everything else work as normal.

Security

Access needs a login for your household. Through the Home Assistant app it sits behind Home Assistant's authenticated HTTPS ingress, with nothing exposed directly to the internet and no ports to forward. Run on its own, it's a small web service to put behind your own reverse proxy and TLS.

The print and share view is a plain page behind the same login, for printing, saving to PDF or screenshotting. There are no public links; what you send someone is the file.
