Privacy & data

Where your data lives

My Kitchen is built to be self-hosted and honest about the one thing that leaves your network.

Stays on your hardware

Your accounts, stock list, ingredient catalogue, saved and generated recipes, history, favourites and notes all live in a single database file on the machine you run My Kitchen on. There's no account with us, no cloud sync, no telemetry, no analytics. Nothing is shared between households — it isn't a community platform.

Leaves your network

When (and only when) you generate a recipe, the app sends a short brief to your chosen LLM provider, using your own API key. It contains:

  • The ingredients in play: what you picked to use up, what's in stock, your staples, and any short notes attached to them.
  • Your equipment list.
  • The meal type, cuisine, time band and number of servings.
  • The dietary tags of the people you're cooking for, as written (the tag names, not the names of the people).
  • The titles of your recent recipes, so you don't get the same thing twice.
  • A randomly chosen creative angle.

That's the whole of it. Your account details, your history beyond those titles, your notes and your favourites are never sent; notes in particular are deliberately kept out of the brief. Nothing is sent at any other time: browsing, filtering, suggestions and stock all happen locally.

This is the deliberate trade-off behind the project: a small Raspberry Pi can't match a hosted model's quality and speed, so generation uses a cloud model while everything else stays local. We'd rather say that plainly than market the app as fully private when a model call is involved.

One caveat worth stating: dietary tags are free text you write yourself. If you name one after a medical condition, that wording goes in the brief. Naming tags after foods rather than diagnoses keeps them out.

Running fully local

Not possible today, and the site shouldn't imply otherwise. The provider adapter is built to be swappable — that's how mock, Gemini and Anthropic already sit behind one interface — and a local model such as Ollama is the natural next one to add. It's on the list rather than in the app. On capable hardware it would give an entirely on-network setup; on a small Pi it would cost you real recipe quality and speed, which is why it hasn't been the priority.

In the meantime, mock mode runs the entire app with no API key and no outbound call at all. You lose real generation, but stock, suggestions, your own recipes and everything else work exactly as normal.

Security

Access is gated by a per-household login on your network. Run via the Home Assistant add-on and it sits behind HA's authenticated, HTTPS ingress — nothing is exposed directly to the internet and there are no ports to forward. Run it standalone and it's an ordinary small web service you put behind your own reverse proxy and TLS.

The print / share view is worth a specific mention, because “share” usually means a public link. Here it doesn't: it's a chrome-free page behind the same login, for printing, saving to PDF or screenshotting. What you send someone is the file, not access to your instance.

This page describes the app's design intent. You are responsible for your own hosting, your LLM provider choice and their terms.
