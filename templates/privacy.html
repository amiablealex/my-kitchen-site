Privacy & data

Where your data lives

My Kitchen is built to be self-hosted and honest about the one thing that leaves your network.

Stays on your hardware

Your accounts, stock list, ingredient catalogue, saved and generated recipes, history and favourites all live in a single database file on the machine you run My Kitchen on. There's no account with us, no cloud sync, no telemetry, no analytics. Nothing is shared between households — it isn't a community platform.

Leaves your network

When — and only when — you generate a recipe, the app sends a short brief to your chosen LLM provider: the ingredients in play, your constraints (meal type, time, dietary needs as written) and a creative angle. It's low-sensitivity by design, but it does go to that provider under their terms, using your own API key.

{{ icon('info') }}
This is the deliberate trade-off behind the project: a small Raspberry Pi can't match a hosted model's quality and speed, so generation uses a cloud model while everything else stays local. We'd rather say that plainly than market the app as fully private when a model call is involved.

Running fully local

If you'd rather nothing left your network at all, the provider adapter is designed to accept a local model (such as Ollama) behind the same interface. On capable hardware that gives you an entirely on-network setup; on a small Pi you'll trade some recipe quality and speed for it. The choice is yours.

Security

Access is gated by a per-household login on your network. Run via the Home Assistant add-on and it sits behind HA's authenticated, HTTPS ingress — nothing is exposed directly to the internet and there are no ports to forward. Run it standalone and it's an ordinary small web service you put behind your own reverse proxy and TLS.

This page describes the app's design intent. You are responsible for your own hosting, your LLM provider choice and their terms.
