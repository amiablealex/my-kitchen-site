Overview

My Kitchen keeps three things: what's in stock, a catalogue of ingredients, and the household's recipes. Every ingredient line in a recipe is linked to an entry in the catalogue, so the app can check a recipe against your stock and tell you what you can cook now.

AI is one way of getting recipes, not the core of the app. It's used when you ask for new recipes, ask for a new version of one, ask @kitchen a question, scan a receipt, or import a recipe. Everything else, including suggestions, runs locally with no AI involved.

Stock

Each ingredient is either in stock or not. The Stock screen shows what's in as chips, grouped and coloured by section. Tap an item to add or change a short note (“needs using”, “1 large”, “half a bag”), which goes into the brief when you generate. Tap × when you've run out; Undo puts it back.

The search box at the top filters what's in stock and, underneath, shows matching ingredients from your catalogue as dashed chips. Tap one to add it. If nothing in the catalogue matches, you can add it as a new ingredient there and then.

Adding several at once

Type or paste a list into the same box, such as “milk, eggs, leeks” or an online order email, and it offers Add these N items. A sheet shows every line, grouped: Add to stock (ticked; untick anything you don't want), Needs a hand, Already have (in stock or a staple) and Skipped. Tap Mark as in stock to save; Undo puts it all back.

The list is matched on your own machine first. Lines it can't place go to the AI as text, just those lines, and anything still unknown waits under Needs a hand rather than being dropped. Tap any line to change it: pick the right ingredient from your catalogue, add it as a new one, or skip the line. It then moves to the right group by itself.

Used anything up?

At the end of a recipe's method, Finished? Mark anything you've used up opens the same sheet with that recipe's ingredients that are in stock, none ticked. Tap what's run out.

{{ icon('info') }}
Stock has no quantities, no counting down as you cook and no low-stock alerts. A count needs updating every time you cook and soon goes wrong; an in-or-out toggle stays right. More on that.

Receipts

The receipt icon in the stock search box reads a photo of a shopping receipt. Take or choose a photo, then drag the two bars so that only the item lines are between them. Only that part is sent: the photo is cropped and shrunk on your phone before it's uploaded, and it isn't kept. What the AI finds opens in the same sheet as a pasted list, with every line it read and the receipt's own text, so you can check it against the paper: things you already have are shown faded rather than hidden, and non-food lines are folded under Skipped. Tap any line the AI got wrong to change it. Lines it couldn't match show its guess (“Dragon fruit?”) under Needs a hand, ready to add as a new ingredient with the category filled in.

A receipt can carry the shop, the date and time, and card and loyalty details, which is why framing comes first. If you'd rather not send receipt photos at all, pasting the receipt's text works too, or turn the feature off with receipt_scanning. More on Privacy & data.

Receipt scanning only appears when your AI model can read images. Receipt reads and list matches have their own daily limit.

Ingredients & the catalogue

Each catalogue ingredient has a category (Protein, Carbohydrate, Vegetable, Dairy, Spice, Oil, Pantry, Herb, Fruit, Condiment). Categories fall into four sections, protein, carb, veg and other, which set the colours and the lanes in the wizard. A new install starts with about 170 ingredients; rename, retire and add your own under Manage.

An ingredient is either:

  • A core ingredient: something that comes and goes, checked in and out on the Stock screen.
  • A staple: oil, salt, common spices, flour. Assumed always available and kept off the stock list. Staples are managed under Manage.

Ingredient links

When a recipe is saved, each ingredient line is matched to a catalogue entry. The matching is plain code, not AI: normalising the text, a list of synonyms, and fuzzy matching above a confidence threshold. A line it isn't confident about is left unlinked rather than guessed.

Each ingredient on a recipe shows its link as a small tag. Edit links lets you re-link a line, unlink it, or add a new catalogue ingredient and link it in one go. Your corrections are shared with the household and aren't changed behind your back.

Imported recipes keep each line's own wording (“2 garlic cloves, crushed”), linked where the app recognises the ingredient. A line can stay unlinked and be linked later, but until it is, the recipe can't be checked against your stock and won't be suggested. A note under Ingredients says so, with Link it to fix it. Staples such as water and salt are worth having in your catalogue for this reason.

Equipment

Your equipment list (air fryer, wok, large baking dish) is under Manage. It goes into the brief, so recipes are written for what you have. Something out of action can be marked unavailable instead of deleted.

The Lets Cook wizard

One question per screen. Back and Continue sit together at the bottom right on every step.

  1. Ingredients to use up. Tap in-stock items to make them must-use; anything generated will include them. Leave it empty and it cooks from whatever's in. Staples are in their own drawer, so you can build a meal around one if you want.
  2. Meal type and cuisine. Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack, Side dish, Dessert, Baking, or Sauce or dressing. Cuisine is only asked where it applies (not for desserts, for example), and can stay on Any.
  3. Time. Quick (under 30 minutes) or Relaxed (30–75).
  4. Who's eating. Pick people from the household and add a number of guests. This sets the servings and the dietary needs. Each person's tags show next to their name.
  5. Ready to cook? Your brief as a row of chips (tap Full brief for the detail), then any saved recipes you can cook now, then Make something new.

Stock is read as you go, so fixing it part-way through (Stock not right? Update) shows up straight away.

Suggestions

On Ready to cook?, Already in your recipes lists saved recipes that fit the brief and can be cooked with what's in. A recipe is included when:

  • every required ingredient is linked to something in stock (staples count as in);
  • its meal type matches, and its cuisine matches unless you chose Any;
  • every must-use ingredient you picked is in it.

Optional extras don't count against a recipe. The time band is shown but doesn't filter. It's a database query, so it's instant and costs nothing.

The home page runs the same check without a brief, and says how many saved recipes you could make right now. Tap the line to see them.

{{ icon('info') }}
If a required ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue, the recipe is left out rather than offered on a guess. Expect few suggestions at first; they grow with your recipes. The Unlinked ingredients filter on Recipes lists the ones to fix.

New recipes

Under Make something new you can add a request (“something spicy”, “a one-pot”, up to 280 characters) and tap Generate recipes. The brief goes to your provider, which returns two different recipes to choose between.

The brief contains your must-use picks, everything else in stock with its notes, your staples and equipment, the meal type, cuisine, time and servings, the dietary tags of whoever's eating, your request, the titles of your recent recipes (to avoid repeats) and one randomly chosen creative angle. The angle applies to the first recipe only; the second is written as a different alternative.

Recipes use metric units and British ingredient names. The model can suggest up to two things you don't have, marked Optional extra, never something the dish depends on.

Generation runs in the background. A waiting page updates itself and moves on to the two recipes when they're ready, so a slow response can't time out, even over a remote connection. Tapping generate twice joins the same wait rather than starting another.

Choosing

Take your pick shows the two recipes numbered 1 and 2, with their cuisine, time and servings. Tap Cook this on one. Both are saved either way, so the other stays in your recipes.

If neither appeals, Neither one? asks for two more from the same brief, told which two you passed on, with an optional steer. You can do this up to three times per brief (see limits), and go back to the previous pair from there.

Recipes & favourites

Every recipe has the same parts, whoever wrote it: intro, ingredients, prep, cook and optional tips. A small petal marks where it came from: four colours for an AI-generated recipe, lime for one written by hand and orange for an imported one.

The Recipes list is one list, newest first, with each recipe on two lines beside its petal: the title, then its meal, cuisine, cook, when and whether it's quick or relaxed. A pair you haven't chosen between yet shows as one Pick one row.

The search box does the organising. The filter button at its end shows every filter at once: who made it, meal, cuisine and cook. Tap it again to close them. One tap adds a filter, so your own recipes are two taps away, and filters stack (“By hand” and “Dessert”). Typing searches titles and offers any filter that matches what you type. Type an ingredient's name, say “brown sugar”, and recipes that use it show too, with the count reading “3 recipes use brown sugar”. This only finds ingredient lines that are linked. A count shows while you're filtering, and the filter is kept when you open a recipe and come back. Favourites works the same way.

When any recipe has an ingredient line that isn't linked, the filters gain a last group, Needs a hand, with Unlinked ingredients. Pick it for a to-do list: open each recipe, use Link it, and come back. Linked recipes drop off the list, and the group disappears once there's nothing left to link.

Favourites are per person. Meal type and cuisine can be changed on any recipe with Edit tags, which matters because they decide whether it appears in suggestions.

Archive

The ⋯ menu on a recipe has Archive this recipe. Archived recipes are hidden from your recipes, favourites and suggestions, but nothing is deleted: Recipes → Archive lists them with a Restore button. A failed generation in the list has a small × to clear it.

Servings

The stepper above a recipe's ingredients scales the amounts for any number of servings. This happens on your screen only: nothing is saved, and the print view always uses the recipe's own count. The method isn't changed, so quantities and times in the steps stay as written. To have those rewritten too, tap Rewrite the method for this many, which opens a new version with that change filled in.

Versions

An AI-generated recipe can be changed by asking for a new version. Tap + New version at the end of the version trail at the top of the recipe, describe the change (“make it lighter”, “swap the broccoli for green beans”), or tap one of the suggested changes, then Create.

Before anything is sent, a confirmation shows your change and explains that the AI will rewrite the recipe as the next version and the current one is kept. Only the newest version can be changed again. Each version in the trail is labelled with the change that made it, and an earlier version shows a link back to the latest.

A new version keeps the original's meal type and who's eating, with their current dietary tags. It counts towards the daily generation limit.

Notes & @kitchen

Any recipe can have notes: how it went, what you changed, whether to make it again. They're dated and attributed, oldest first, and the whole household sees them. Notes can't be edited (add another one instead), and you can delete your own. Recipes with notes show a speech bubble in the list. Notes are never sent to the AI.

Asking @kitchen

Start a note with @kitchen, or tap Ask @kitchen, to ask a question about the recipe: what to swap, how long it keeps, how to tell when it's done. The reply appears under the question in the thread, usually within a few seconds. A slower one shows Thinking…, and a failed one has Try again.

The model is given the recipe, the servings you're viewing it at, the last few questions and answers on that recipe, what's in stock, your staples, and the allergies and dislikes of everyone in the household, so it won't suggest something someone can't eat. It answers questions; it doesn't rewrite the recipe.

Make this version N

When an answer suggests a change big enough to need the recipe rewriting (making it vegetarian, cooking it in a slow cooker, turning it into a traybake), a Make this version N button appears under the reply. It opens the new-version panel with the change filled in, and the same confirmation applies. Small swaps the cook can make as they go (lemon for lime, dried herbs for fresh) and questions about storage, timing or technique don't get the button.

Questions use a lighter, cheaper model by default, and have their own daily limit.

Adding recipes

Recipes you add sit alongside the generated ones and are offered in suggestions in the same way, so a family recipe or one from a book comes up when you have the ingredients. Add a recipe on the Recipes page offers three ways in.

Write it yourself

You fill in the same sections as any other recipe and pick ingredients from your catalogue, so every line is linked when you save. Ingredient, prep, cook and tip rows can be dragged into order. Set the meal type, cuisine and time band so it can be suggested. Edit it later from the recipe's ⋯ menu.

Import from the web

Paste a link to a recipe. Most recipe sites include a structured copy of the recipe in the page, and when there is one, only that is sent to the AI; otherwise the page's text is. You can also paste a recipe's text instead, for a site that won't share its page or a recipe from a message.

The AI lays it out as ingredients, prep, cook and tips without rewriting it: the source's wording, quantities and method are kept, and only US cups and °F are converted. It picks the meal type, cuisine and time. The result opens in the recipe form, headed Check the import, and nothing is saved until you press Save recipe. The recipe then shows where it came from (“from bbcgoodfood.com”), on its page and when printed.

From a photo

Photograph a cookbook page or a handwritten card. A recipe that runs over the page is fine: add up to four pages and they're read together in one go, so ingredients on one page and the method on the next come back as one recipe. If a page shows more than one recipe, say which you want. The photos are shrunk on your phone and aren't kept; the recipe shows “from a photo”. This option only appears when your AI model can read images.

Web and text imports use the lighter @kitchen model; photos use the main model, which reads handwriting better. Imports count towards the household's daily limit.

Print & share

Print or share in a recipe's ⋯ menu opens a plain version of the recipe, without navigation, that prints or saves to PDF on a white background and screenshots in one piece.

A link to your instance won't work for anyone outside your network, so this gives you a file to send instead. There's no public link: the page is behind the same login as the rest of the app.

Household & diets

Add the people in your house under Household, each with a login, favourites and dietary tags. In the wizard, the tags of whoever you're cooking for go into the brief. Guests add servings but have no dietary tags.

There are three kinds of tag:

TypeWhat it meansExamples
Allergy Never used, in any form, anywhere in the recipe. Overrides everything else. Peanuts, shellfish
Dislike Kept out of the whole recipe: ingredients, optional extras, tips and serving suggestions. A matter of taste, not safety. Pesto, olives, blue cheese
Diet A general way of eating that shapes the whole recipe. Vegetarian, gluten-free, nothing too spicy

When they conflict

If you pick chicken to use up while cooking for a vegetarian, something has to give. The order, strongest first:

  1. Allergies. A selected ingredient that's an allergen for someone eating is dropped.
  2. Ingredients you chose in the wizard.
  3. Dislikes, unless you picked that ingredient yourself.
  4. Diets, which shape the recipe but don't override a deliberate pick.

A tag only reaches the brief if it's assigned to someone who's eating. You can assign it to people when you create it.

{{ icon('info') }}
Allergies: an AI is not a safety guarantee. Allergies go into the brief as absolute exclusions and the obvious cases are handled well, but the app can't check the output, because there's no allergen data behind the tags. Recipes generated with an allergy in the brief show a reminder to check the ingredients yourself. Always do.

Usage & limits

The Usage page (in the menu) shows today's counts against the limits, for you and the household, totals over recent days, a per-person breakdown and the most recent calls, each with its model, tokens and an estimated cost.

The limits reset at midnight in your timezone. New recipes, new versions and retries share the per-person generation limit; @kitchen questions have their own; receipt reads and list matches have their own; imports count only towards the household; and every call counts towards the household total. The defaults and settings are under Costs and limits.

Self-hosting & data

Accounts, stock, the catalogue, recipes, history, favourites and notes are all in one SQLite database file on your machine. There's no account with me, no sync and no telemetry.

Only the AI requests leave your network, sent to your chosen provider with your own key, and only when you generate, ask for a new version, ask @kitchen, scan a receipt, match a pasted list or import a recipe. What each one contains is on Privacy & data.

Your own model

{{ icon('info') }}
Untested. I've tried to let you use your own model, but I haven't been able to test it with one. It will only be as good as your model.

Set llm_provider to openai_compatible to use any server that speaks the OpenAI chat API: Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM, or a cloud service such as OpenRouter. Give it the server's address and the model's name; the settings are under Configuration.

Recipe quality was tuned on Gemini. Smaller models write plainer recipes and may break the format, which shows as a failed generation. They also follow allergy and dislike rules less reliably, and the prompt is the only safeguard, so check recipes for anyone with an allergy. A Raspberry Pi is too slow to run a useful model itself; a computer with a recent graphics card or Apple Silicon is a better host. Turn on llm_images only if the model reads images; otherwise receipts and photo imports don't appear.

Troubleshooting

My Kitchen isn't in the Home Assistant app store

The store caches its list. Use Settings → Apps → ⋮ → Check for updates, or reload the Supervisor, and look again. Apps also need Home Assistant OS or Supervised; they aren't available on Container or Core.

I've lost the first-run password

It's printed once in the app's Log tab. If it's gone, run flask set-password from the app's shell to set a new one.

The sidebar entry hasn't changed after an update

Changes to the sidebar panel need a full Home Assistant Core restart; restarting the app isn't enough.

The app looks out of date after an update

The app caches its styles on your phone. Close it fully and open it again.

Generation fails or returns nothing

Check the provider and API key on the Configuration tab, and the app's log for the error. You may also have reached a daily limit; the Usage page shows where you are. Setting the provider to mock is a quick way to check the rest of the app is working.

There's no receipt icon, or no “From a photo”

Both need an AI model that reads images. Gemini and Anthropic do; with your own model, turn on llm_images if yours does. The receipt icon also needs receipt_scanning on.

An import says the site wouldn't share the page

Some sites turn away requests that don't come from a browser. Open the recipe yourself, copy it, and use Or paste the recipe's text instead.

A recipe doesn't show up in suggestions

Usually one of three things: an ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue (the recipe says so under Ingredients, and the Unlinked ingredients filter on Recipes lists every such recipe), the recipe has no meal type, or a required ingredient isn't in stock.

“Add to home screen” installs Home Assistant, not My Kitchen

Through Home Assistant's ingress, the browser sees Home Assistant rather than My Kitchen, and Home Assistant doesn't provide a URL that opens an app directly, so a separate install isn't possible that way.

Something else? Open an issue on GitHub.
