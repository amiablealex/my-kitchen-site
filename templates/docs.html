How it works

A light tour of the ideas behind My Kitchen and how to use it day to day. For installing and configuring, see Get started.

The big idea

My Kitchen is the household's recipe brain: it knows what's in stock, all of your recipes, and turns that knowledge into “what's cookable right now?”

Stock

Stock is a simple in-or-out toggle which you maintain from the Stock screen: a grouped view of what's in, search-to-add against your catalogue, and a tap to remove. Quick notes like “needs using” or “1 large” can ride along and get passed to the model for generations.

Ingredients & the catalogue

Behind the app is a catalogue of ingredients grouped into categories (protein, carb, veg and more). Each ingredient is either:

  • A core ingredient: things that come and go, which you maintain in the stock manager.
  • A staple: oil, salt, common spices, flour. Assumed always available and used freely; you don't need to track these.

When a recipe is saved, each ingredient line is matched to a catalogue entry by a deterministic resolver. On any recipe you'll see the linked ingredient, and you can re-link, unlink or add-and-link a line yourself if the app ever gets it wrong.

The Lets Cook wizard

This is where the app helps you decvide what to make right now.

  1. Choose ingredients to use up. Tap in-stock items to make them must-use. Leave it blank to cook from whatever's in.
  2. Meal type & cuisine. Breakfast through to a sauce or dressing; cuisine where it applies (e.g mediterranean, british...).
  3. Time. Quick (under 30 min) or relaxed (30–75 min).
  4. Cooking for. Pick people and add guests; this is how the app will calculate servings and dietary needs.

Stock is read live as you go, so a mid-flow stock fix shows up immediately.

Suggestions

Before any generation, the review step shows saved recipes that are cookable right now that fit your brief. A recipe qualifies when all of its required ingredients are linked to something in stock, the meal type and cuisine match, and any must-use ingredients appear in it.

{{ icon('info') }}
Suggestions are precise on purpose: if a required ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue, the recipe is left out rather than promised falsely. Sparse at first, it grows as your history does.

Generation

Nothing takes your fancy? An integrated AI model takes the brief and a creative angle, then returns two distinctly different recipes for you to choose from with an energetic write-up e.g technique, doneness cues, tips, finishing touches.

Recipes & favourites

Every recipe fits into the same standardised stricture: intro, ingredients, prep, cook, tips. Recipes are grouped by source: currently AI-generated, and ones you write yourself.

The Recipes screen is a dated history you can filter by cook and by source. Favouriting any recipe makes it appear into a separate smaller list. Each registered user has their own favourites, whereas all users see every recipe created by anyone in the main recipe history list.

Household & diets

Add the people in your house, each with their own dietary preferences and allergies. In the wizard, if you specify who you're cooking for, the brief combines their needs: allergies are stated as absolute exclusions and preferences as gentle steers.

{{ icon('info') }}
Allergies: an AI is not a safety guarantee. Recipes generated with an allergy in the brief carry a visible reminder to check the ingredients yourself. Always do.

Self-hosting & data

Everything runs on your hardware and your data stays there. The only thing that leaves your network is the generation brief; a short, low-sensitivity description of ingredients and constraints sent to your chosen LLM provider. A local-model would give a fully local setup. See more: Privacy & data.
