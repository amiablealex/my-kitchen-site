How it works

A light tour of the ideas behind My Kitchen and how to use it day to day. For installing and configuring, see Get started.

The big idea

My Kitchen is the household's recipe brain: it knows what's in stock, all of your recipes, and turns that knowledge into “what's cookable right now?”

Stock

Stock is a simple in-or-out toggle. There are no quantities, no “mark as cooked” decrement, no low-stock alerts — deliberately. Inventory systems are what kill household tools through maintenance burden, so stock stays a binary toggle, forever.

You maintain it from the Stock screen: a grouped view of what's in, search-to-add against your catalogue, and a tap to remove. Quick notes like “needs using” or “1 large” can ride along and get passed to the model.

Ingredients & the catalogue

Behind the app is a catalogue of ingredients grouped into categories (protein, carb, veg and more). Each one is either:

  • A core ingredient — the things that come and go, which you stock and choose between.
  • A staple — oil, salt, common spices, flour. Assumed always available and used freely; you don't track these.

When a recipe is saved, each ingredient line is matched to a catalogue entry by a deterministic resolver (no AI guesswork). On any recipe you'll see a calm pill naming the linked ingredient, and you can re-link, unlink or add-and-link a line yourself — the app never silently overrides a manual link.

The Lets Cook wizard

A short, linear flow — one screen at a time, easy to step back, built to use one-handed at the hob:

  1. Choose ingredients to use up. Tap in-stock items to make them must-use. Leave it blank to cook from whatever's in.
  2. Meal type & cuisine. Breakfast through to a sauce or dressing; cuisine where it applies.
  3. Time. Quick (under 30 min) or relaxed (30–75 min).
  4. Cooking for. Pick people and add guests — servings and dietary needs follow automatically.

Stock is read live as you go, so a mid-flow stock fix shows up immediately.

Suggestions

Before any generation, the review step shows saved recipes you can cook right now that fit your brief — as a row of cards. A recipe qualifies when every required ingredient is linked to something in stock (staples assumed), the meal type and cuisine match, and any must-use picks appear in it.

Suggestions are precise on purpose: if a required ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue, the recipe is left out rather than promised falsely. Sparse at first — it grows as your history does.

Generation

Nothing takes your fancy? Generate. The app builds a brief from your stock, constraints and a randomly chosen creative angle, then returns two genuinely different recipes with an energetic write-up — the why, the technique, doneness cues, finishing touches. You pick one; both are saved.

Generation runs in the background, so a slow model never ties up the page — you wait on a simple progress screen and land on the choice when it's ready.

Recipes & favourites

Every recipe renders the same four-part way: intro, ingredients, prep, cook (plus tips when there are any). Recipes come from three sources — AI-generated, ones you write yourself, and (later) imported — each marked with a small colour-coded dot.

The Recipes screen is a dated history you can filter by cook and by source. Favourite any recipe, from any source; favourites are per-person, and they feed back into your cookable suggestions.

Household & diets

Add the people in your house, each with their own dietary preferences and allergies. When you choose who you're cooking for, the brief combines their needs: allergies are stated as absolute exclusions, preferences as gentle steers.

{{ icon('info') }}
Allergies: an AI is not a safety guarantee. Recipes generated with an allergy in the brief carry a visible reminder to check the ingredients yourself. Always do.

Self-hosting & data

Everything runs on your hardware and your data stays there. The only thing that leaves your network is the generation brief — a short, low-sensitivity description of ingredients and constraints — sent to your chosen LLM provider. A local-model option exists for a fully on-network setup. The full picture is on the Privacy & data page.

Troubleshooting

The sidebar panel doesn't reflect a setting change

Some Home Assistant panel changes need a full Core restart, not just an add-on restart.

Generation seems to hang

It runs in the background and the page polls for the result; very long generations are expected to finish on the progress screen. If a run errors, the screen offers a retry.

My catalogue didn't change after an update

A normal update keeps your existing catalogue and only applies additive migrations. Rebuilding the seed catalogue is a deliberate step — see the repo's notes before doing it, as it clears data.
