The basic idea

My Kitchen is the household's recipe brain. It knows what's in stock and it holds all of your recipes — the ones you've written and the ones it has generated — and it turns that into “what's cookable right now?”

Two things follow from that. Recipes aren't stored as loose text: every ingredient line is linked to a real entry in your ingredient catalogue, which is what makes “can I make this tonight?” a question the app can actually answer. And AI generation is one source of recipes among several, not the whole product — it's what you reach for when nothing you already have takes your fancy.

Stock

Stock is a simple in-or-out toggle, maintained from the Stock screen: a view of what's in, grouped and colour-coded by section, one search box that filters what you already have and adds new things from your catalogue, and a tap to remove.

Each item can carry a short note — “needs using”, “1 large”, “half a bag” — which is passed to the model when you generate.

{{ icon('info') }}
Stock is in-or-out and always will be. There are no quantities, no decrementing when you cook, no low-stock alerts. Keeping an inventory accurate is the chore that kills household tools, and a toggle you'll actually maintain beats a count you won't. More on that thinking ›

Ingredients & the catalogue

Behind the app is a catalogue of ingredients, each in a category (Protein, Carbohydrate, Vegetable, Dairy, Spice, Oil, Pantry, Herb, Fruit, Condiment). Categories map onto four sections — protein, carb, veg and other — which is what drives the colour coding and the lanes in the wizard. A fresh install seeds roughly 170 ingredients to start you off; rename, retire and add your own freely under Manage.

Every ingredient is one of two things:

  • A core ingredient. Things that come and go, which you check in and out on the Stock screen.
  • A staple. Oil, salt, common spices, flour. Assumed always available and used freely, so they stay out of the stock list entirely — you manage them separately under Manage.

Ingredient links

When a recipe is saved, each ingredient line is matched to a catalogue entry by a deterministic resolver — no AI involved, just normalisation, a synonym list and fuzzy matching above a confidence threshold. Anything it can't match confidently is left unmatched rather than guessed at.

On any recipe, each ingredient shows the catalogue entry it's linked to as a small tag. Tap Edit links to re-link a line, unlink it, or add a brand-new ingredient to your catalogue and link it in one step. Your corrections are shared across the household and stick — nothing is silently re-linked behind your back.

Equipment

Also under Manage is your equipment list — air fryer, wok, large baking dish, whatever you've got. It's passed to the model with the brief, so recipes are written for the kitchen you actually have. Anything temporarily out of action can be marked unavailable rather than deleted.

The Lets Cook wizard

This is where the app helps you decide what to make right now. Each step is one screen and you can go back at any point.

  1. Choose ingredients to use up. Tap in-stock items to mark them must-use — whatever comes out will be built to include them. Leave it blank and it cooks from whatever's in. Staples live in their own drawer below the lanes, so you can build a meal around the oregano if that's the mood; picking a staple is creative direction, not a statement about availability.
  2. Meal type & cuisine. Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack, Side dish, Dessert, Baking, or a Sauce or dressing. Meal type comes first and gates cuisine — a dessert doesn't ask you to pick between Italian and Mexican. Where cuisine applies you can leave it on Any.
  3. Time. Quick (under 30 minutes) or Relaxed (30–75).
  4. Who's eating. Pick people from your household and add a guest count. This is how servings and dietary needs are worked out. Each person's tags show as colour-coded badges, so an allergy is visible at the moment you apply it.
  5. Review. A recap of the brief, then any saved recipes you could cook right now (below), and finally the button to generate something new.

Stock is read live as you go, so a mid-flow fix via the “Stock not right? Update →” link shows up immediately.

Suggestions

Before any generation, the review step surfaces saved recipes that are cookable right now and fit your brief, as tappable cards. This is the part that makes the app more than a generator: the bank you've built up gets offered back to you at the moment it's useful.

A recipe qualifies when all of these hold:

  • Every required ingredient is linked to something that's currently in stock (staples are assumed).
  • Its meal type matches, and its cuisine matches unless you chose Any.
  • Every must-use ingredient you picked actually appears in it.

Optional extras don't count against a recipe, and the time band is shown as a badge but never used to filter. It's a plain database query — no AI, no cost, no waiting.

{{ icon('info') }}
Suggestions are precise on purpose: if a required ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue, the recipe is left out rather than promised falsely. Expect this to be sparse at first and to grow as your recipe bank does.

Generation

Nothing takes your fancy? Generation sends a short brief to your chosen model and returns two deliberately different recipes to choose between, each with an energetic write-up: why the dish works, technique, doneness cues you can see and smell, and finishing touches worth bothering with.

The brief carries your must-use picks, everything else in stock, your staples and equipment, meal type, cuisine, time band, servings, the dietary needs of whoever's eating, the titles of your recent recipes (so you don't get the same thing twice), and one randomly chosen creative angle. That angle is applied to the first recipe only — the second is designed freely as a genuine alternative, differing in technique or flavour direction.

Recipes are written in UK units and British ingredient names throughout. The model may suggest up to two things you don't have, flagged as Optional Extra — a garnish or a flavour lift, never something the meal is built around.

Generation runs in the background. You get a “cooking up your recipes…” page that updates itself and jumps to your two options the moment they're ready, so a slow generation can't time out on you — including over a remote connection. Tapping generate twice resumes the same wait rather than starting a second one.

Both recipes are saved, not just the one you pick, so the road not taken stays retrievable from your history.

Recipes & favourites

Every recipe has the same shape whatever its origin: intro, ingredients, prep, cook, and optional tips. A small coloured dot marks where it came from — a pinwheel for AI-generated, a solid dot for one you wrote.

The Recipes screen is a dated list of everything the household has. One search box filters it by cuisine, meal type, cook and source as well as by name: start typing, tap a suggestion, and it becomes a chip you can stack or remove. Filtering happens instantly as you type, which matters when you're at the hob.

Favouriting is per person — everyone sees every recipe in the main list, but your starred list is yours. Meal type and cuisine tags can be edited inline on any recipe, which is worth doing: those tags are what make a recipe show up in suggestions later.

Writing your own recipes

Recipes you write yourself sit alongside the generated ones, and they're arguably the more valuable half of the bank — a family recipe or a favourite from a book becomes something the app can offer you when you've got the ingredients in.

Create one from the Recipes page. You fill in the same sections the app uses everywhere, and pick ingredients straight from your catalogue, so the lines are linked from the moment you save — no matching needed. Ingredient, prep, cook and tip rows can be dragged into order within their sections. Tag the meal type, cuisine and time band and it'll be offered up in the wizard like any other recipe.

{{ icon('info') }}
Importing a recipe from a web page or a photograph is the next thing on the list. Today it's typed in by hand.

Notes

Any recipe can carry notes: how it went, what you changed, whether to make it again. They're attributed and dated, oldest first, and everyone in the house sees them — it's a shared cooking log rather than a private scratchpad.

Cooked it a second time? Add a second note. There's no editing — a correction is just another note, which keeps the log honest — and you can delete your own. Recipes with notes show a small speech bubble in the list.

Notes are never fed back into generation. They're for you to read before cooking something again, not a signal the model gets to interpret.

Print & share

Every recipe has a Print / share this recipe action. It opens a clean version with no navigation or app furniture, which prints — or saves to PDF — properly on a light background, and screenshots in one piece.

This exists because the obvious thing to do, pasting someone a 192.168.… link, doesn't work outside your network. The share view hands you an artifact instead: a PDF or an image you send however you like. There's no public link and nothing is exposed — the page sits behind the same login as everything else.

Household & diets

Add the people in your house under Household, each with their own login, favourites and dietary tags. In the wizard, whoever you're cooking for has their tags combined into the brief. Guests add servings but carry no dietary data.

Tags come in three kinds, and the difference matters — it's the difference between “never, under any circumstances” and “they're not keen”:

TypeWhat it meansExamples
Allergy Never used, in any form, anywhere in the recipe. Overrides everything else. Peanuts, shellfish
Dislike A food someone doesn't like. Kept out of the whole recipe — ingredients, optional extras, tips and serving suggestions alike. Taste, not safety. Pesto, olives, blue cheese
Diet How someone eats generally. Steers the whole recipe rather than banning one thing. Vegetarian, gluten-free, nothing too spicy

When things conflict

Pick chicken to use up while cooking for a vegetarian and something has to give. The order is fixed, strongest first:

  1. Allergies win outright. If an ingredient you selected is an allergen for someone at the table, it's dropped rather than used.
  2. Ingredients you chose in the wizard come next. A deliberate pick is treated as deliberate.
  3. Dislikes are excluded from everything — unless you explicitly picked the thing, in which case you clearly meant it.
  4. Diets steer the whole recipe, but never against something you chose on purpose.

A tag only reaches a brief if it's assigned to someone, and only when that person is one of the people you're cooking for. You can assign a tag to people as you create it.

{{ icon('info') }}
Allergies: an AI is not a safety guarantee. Allergies are stated in the brief as absolute exclusions and the model handles the obvious cases well, but the app can't verify the output in code — there's no ingredient-level allergen data behind the tags. Recipes generated with an allergy in play carry a visible reminder to check the ingredients yourself. Always do.

Self-hosting & data

Everything runs on your hardware and your data stays there: accounts, stock, catalogue, recipes, history, favourites and notes all live in a single database file on your machine. There's no account with us, no sync and no telemetry.

The one thing that leaves your network is the generation brief — a short, low-sensitivity description of ingredients and constraints, sent to your chosen provider with your own API key, and only when you press generate. Nothing else is sent, and nothing is sent at any other time. See Privacy & data.

Troubleshooting

I can't see My Kitchen in the Home Assistant 'App' store

The store caches its list. Settings → Apps → ⋮ → Check for updates, or reload the Supervisor, then look again. Apps also need Home Assistant OS or Supervised, they aren't available on Container or Core installs.

I've lost the first-run password

It's printed once in the app's Log tab. If it's scrolled away, running flask set-password from the app's shell sets a new one.

The sidebar entry hasn't changed after an update

Changes to the sidebar panel need a full Home Assistant Core restart. Restarting the app alone isn't enough.

Generation fails or returns nothing

Check the provider and API key on the Configuration tab, and the app log for the error. Switching the provider to mock is a quick way to test if the app itself is fine.

A recipe won't show up in suggestions

Most often one of three things: an ingredient line isn't linked to your catalogue (open the recipe and check the tags beside each ingredient), the recipe has no meal type tag, or one of its required ingredients isn't in stock. Suggestions exclude anything they can't verify.

“Add to home screen” installs Home Assistant, not the My Kitchen app

Through Home Assistant ingress, the browser sees HA's overall app rather than My Kitchen. HA doesn't provide a cloud URL that directs straight to an 'app', so unfortunately a native 'install' is not possible.

Still stuck? Open an issue on the GitHub repository.
